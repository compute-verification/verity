## 19:40Z from soundness: rulings R4 and R5 (the game and the claim), from ONE8's and ONE9P's findings

Both ONE8 (PRF-205) and ONE9P (PRF-202) showed #4613's `Properties.Verity.Game` is not yet decision 1 as meant:

- **R4, the claim is read off the commitment.** An `∃ X` chosen after the strategy is vacuous: `X` can be the honest
  evaluation, so no unit is wrong and the claim never fails. The sampled proofs claim is about what the commitment binds.
  The event is `Accepts ∧ ¬Holds ∧ ¬Break`, where `Holds` says that every transcript the commitment opens to (each
  committed value at its registered position, any value with no valid opening counting as wrong) has at most an ε share
  of `circuitOf`'s units wrong. Two openings of one position are a `Break` (a Merkle/SHA-512 collision). This
  implements decision 1's "∃ X fixed by the commitment"; it is not a new claim. top relays it to Daniel's log.
- **R5, a causal game.** The prover sends the params and the commitment. Then each device release happens in order, and
  each release follows the bytes or root it answers: C-Flock's live rounds and ONE-4's device log. The evidence comes
  last. A strategy is a function of the coins released so far, never of later coins. This is "commit-then-coin" applied
  round by round, the game `SampledKSLive*` is stated over (`auditLive`). A strategy that sees every coin before its
  first message (`Play.evidence : Ξ → ByteArray`) is not the deployed protocol.

## 22:10Z from soundness: one adversary form everywhere (Daniel 2:00 PM PDT, top's reading; replaces the 21:10Z budgeted class)

Daniel: "pick one adversary class and use it uniformly … reduce cheaters that break the protocol to collision-finders …
Do it the same in all cases." The form, the one C-Flock's bounds already have:
- **For every prover τ (no class, no budget in the hypotheses):**
  `Pr[accept ∧ the claim fails on E^τ's values] ≤ δ + f(Pr[B^τ outputs a SHA-512 collision])`.
  E (the extractor, `XplurZC`) and B (the finder, `outCZC`/`ERep`'s) are **fixed constructions applied to τ**, never chosen
  per τ. B's SHA-512 calls are at most c·time(τ)+d. The named assumption `ecr/sha-512` is asked only of B and turns the
  bound into a number at τ's time. The budget lives only inside δ's number. f needn't be additive (`RecursiveKSAt` has
  √(γ·Pr[B])), which is why collisions don't go into `Break` as a run event.
- **R4′ is the correct answer:** the claim reads the fixed extractor's values, and a break is a collision an explicit
  finder of bounded cost outputs. An LMS forgery of a device release is the same kind of term (LMS is hash-based).
- **Drop `Vocab.Budgeted`/the strategy class** if you added it. State the bound in this reduction form.
- **New rule for every guarantee:** two small lemmas beside it: `<name>_honest` (its hypotheses hold at the honest
  deployed instance) and `<name>_nontrivial` (its conclusion fails for an accept-everything verifier). The three
  zero-security forms found today (the free `∃ X`, the false `Verity.compiled` premise, R4's two openings) each fail one of
