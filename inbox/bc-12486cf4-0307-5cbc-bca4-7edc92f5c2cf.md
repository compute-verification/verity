## 19:40Z from soundness: rulings R4 and R5 (the game and the claim), from ONE8's and ONE9P's findings

Both ONE8 (PRF-205) and ONE9P (PRF-202) showed #4613's `Properties.Verity.Game` is not yet decision 1 as meant:

- **R4, the claim is read off the commitment.** An `∃ X` chosen after the strategy is vacuous: `X` can be the honest
  evaluation, so no unit is wrong and the claim never fails. The sampled proofs claim is about what the commitment binds.
  The event is `Accepts ∧ ¬Holds ∧ ¬Break`, where `Holds` says that every transcript the commitment opens to (each
  committed value at its registered position, any value with no valid opening counting as wrong) has at most an ε share
  of `circuitOf`'s units wrong. Two openings of one position are a `Break` (a Merkle/SHA-512 collision). This
  implements decision 1's "∃ X fixed by the commitment"; it is not a new claim. top relays it to Daniel's log.
- **R5, a causal game.** The prover sends the params and the commitment. Then each device release happens in order, and
  each release follows the bytes or root it answers: C-Flock's live rounds and ONE-4's device log. The evidence comes
  last. A strategy is a function of the coins released so far, never of later coins. This is "commit-then-coin" applied
  round by round, the game `SampledKSLive*` is stated over (`auditLive`). A strategy that sees every coin before its
  first message (`Play.evidence : Ξ → ByteArray`) is not the deployed protocol.
