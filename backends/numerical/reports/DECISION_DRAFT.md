# Decision draft: the fastest practical ZK prover for batched exact BF16 dot products

*Synthesis lane, drafted from `main` at ff651a8 (2026-09-22 ~03:00 PDT) for the coordinator to correct before ~07:30 PDT.
Every number carries its ledger lane and run id; every label is the one on the ledger, never upgraded. Tables between
`<!-- BEGIN TABLE -->` markers are regenerated by `python -m verity_numerical.bench.decision_tables --write`
(`backends/numerical/python/verity_numerical/bench/decision_tables.py`); the prose is not. `[COORDINATOR]` marks a
judgment I did not make. Appendix: `DECISION_DRAFT_appendix.md`.*

The question: which architecture is the fastest practical zero-knowledge prover for large batches of the fixed exact
NVIDIA BF16 dot-product relation (one VU = 96 chained `mma.sync m16n8k16 bf16` transitions, K = 1536, c_0 = +0, final
BF16 RNE), at soundness 2^-128, full batch B = 4096 on the same hardware, with latency (sequential challenge depth x RTT)
as a first-class axis, with honest labels.

## 1. Verdict in five lines

1. **Fastest measured full-batch prover tonight: Candidate B-Ligero** (direct Ligero-style prover in torch): **1.41 s for
   4096 VUs = 0.345 ms/VU, 3.50e7 x native, verifier 0.96 s, depth 3**, on an **NVIDIA L40S**, labelled
   **COMPLETE_ZK_BACKEND** by its lane at 2^-128.05 (union over 25 sub-batches of 170 VUs) -- b-zk r20260922-092404-2a1b.
   Its non-ZK and HVZK variants are 1.37 s and 1.43 s on the same run. The nearest competitor, Candidate A's GPU prover,

   **disputed** (line 4).

anchor-a100 lane (r20260922-080250-cff5): on an A100, HBM-bound kernels scale by bandwidth (2.02x the 4090) but the

## 8. What the architecture space says

CHECKER -> LOOKUPS -> WITNESS -> COMMITMENT/ENCODING -> ZK -> GPU. Per stage, what tonight established and which
direction it triggers or closes (C: binary/word arithmetisation; D: specialised Ligero; E: hybrid GKR + committed
checkpoints; F: better commitment/encoding).

- **CHECKER.** v2 is the largest single win of the night and it is candidate-agnostic: 4.8x on A, 2.6x on B-AIR, 3x
  fewer Ligero rows for B-Ligero (modelled). It was proven equivalent (z3 10/10) before it was priced. Remaining
  headroom per unit is 33 mul / 9 lin / 89 lookups: the lookups now dominate, and v2 got there by replacing every
  *decomposition* (hint + range check + recombine) with a fixed-table lookup whose output the lookup argument binds
  (`checker_min.md` 1-2; only 10 range chunks per unit remain). **Triggers C** (binary/word arithmetisation) only as
  the next step past v2 -- v2 already took the word-table route for align/normalise/decode; C would have to beat
  89 lookups/unit, not 306.
- **LOOKUPS.** LogUp was worth 2.9x on B-AIR (173.24 s limbs -> 60.14 s) and is the cost floor of A (the 3.5M-row
  SHIFT table's multiplicities are 5.14M of A's 109M committed elements per proof at v2, gkr/PROTOCOL.md 14.1). Every
  lookup-path soundness bug tonight (U1/U2, R16,
  2^-93.8 at BabyBear^4) was in the lookup argument, and BabyBear^4 cannot reach 2^-128 with 2^25.7 lookups; ^6 costs
  +43% on B-AIR (22.92 -> 32.80 s). **Closes** "BabyBear^4 at 2^-128" for any LogUp design at this batch size.
- **WITNESS STRATEGY.** A commits everything (26.6k-44.9k elements/VU) and runs 328-355 sequential rounds; B-Ligero
  commits 337k elements/VU (8x A) and runs 3 rounds -- and is 3-18x faster. Commitment *volume* is not the cost; the
  *sequential* structure is (A's sumcheck rounds are launch-bound on the GPU: a-fusion's 10-stream logUp and fused
  rounds recover only 1.5x). **Direction E as "checkpoints every m units" is closed** by `latency.md` 6: m = 2 is depth
  386 at 1.5x work, m = 4 is 746 at 2.5x -- deeper *and* slower. What `latency.md` does keep open for A is the *committed
  cut* family (commit the R16 tree at levels [16, 21] + the depth>=3 checker wires: depth 140 at 1.031x, +20.9%
  committed) -- i.e. the hybrid that survives is "GKR + commit the intermediate lookup-tree levels", not
  "GKR + checkpoint the chain". Either way A keeps its proof size (34-42 MB vs 110-122 MB) and gets to 140, not 3.
- **COMMITMENT / ENCODING.** Hash is solved on GPU (b-commit fused encode + BLAKE3 column hash 46.7 ms for the 20.4 GB
  B-shape codeword on a 4090; anchor-a100: 98.0 ms on the A100, BLAKE3 the best hash there). Ligero rate 1/4 with t=192
  is at the union-bound edge (2^-128.05). Exploiting the *structure* of r^T A (b-lintest: the linear test is a per-row
  scalar times one of M = 3 fixed rows) cut B's 4090 model from 258.7 ms / 6.41e6 to 116.4 ms / 2.89e6 (2.2x,
  r20260922-073017-0924). **Triggers F** only for A (its 33.9 MB
