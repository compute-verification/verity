## What changed

Until now the H100 guarantee rested on one mixed premise, `TTOutTilePearlCH100Rev1 CM` (TT_OUT per audit tile at an
abstract cost model `CM`). Which `CM` it holds at was prose. Following the 8 Oct ruling, the premise is split into two
named assumptions in `security/Definitions/Assumptions/PearlC.lean`, and a proved transfer joins them:

1. `w1-complete-h100` (`Pouw.PearlC.Assumptions.W1CompleteH100 salt CM`, measured). `CM` reaches the oracle only
   through counted queries. Every program `p` of `CM` has a W1 program `p'`, fixed before the oracle and the salt, with
   the same run at no more cost and no more oracle calls (`CostModel.SimulatedBy`). Here W1 is
   `W1.w1Priced W1Device.s8H100Best salt`. `s8H100Best` is the Hopper INT8 setting at the per-MAC ratios of the H100
   whitepaper V1.04 Table 1 and the H200 data sheet (S8 and E4M3 at 1 per MAC, BF16 at 2), with `sha = fetch = 0`.
2. `tt-out-tile-pearl-c-h100-rev1` (`Pouw.PearlC.Assumptions.TTOutTilePearlCH100Rev1S8 salt sem ρ`, conjectured).
   It is `TTOutTilePearlCDevRev1W1 s8H100Best salt devH100 sem ρ`: for every in-domain workload, budget `T ≥ 0`,
   `q ≤ 2^64` and W1 adversary within `(T, q)`,
   `Pr_{H, s}[ T/(1 − 1/400) < Σ_{good tiles g} (1 − ρ)·creditDevRev1(g) ] ≤ δPearlC(q, N)`.

What is proved, in `security/Proofs/Pouw/PearlC/W1Best.lean` and `S8H100Rev1Gap.lean`:

* **The transfer**, `Pouw.ttOutTile_of_simulatedBy`: from `Queries`, `SimulatedBy` and TT_OUT at the simulator, TT_OUT
  holds at `CM`.
* **Zero is the adversary's best case.** By `w1Priced_s8H100_simulatedBy_best`, `s8H100 sha fetch` is simulated by
  `s8H100Best` at any `sha, fetch ≥ 0`, so the conjecture at zero implies it at every nonnegative price
  (`ttOutTilePearlCDevRev1W1_s8H100_of_best`).
* **The new guarantees.** `Pouw.SecurityProofs.PearlC.SampledH100Rev1S8_8192` and `…S8Llama32_1b` take the two named
  assumptions and conclude `GGapSampled` at the existing ε (140237/26090000 and 69533/10736000). Like their predecessors,
  they are frozen (conjecture, no function, hash).

## What to attack

Rate each item and say which, if any, you can break:

1. **The conjecture over W1 itself.** Find a W1 program on `s8H100Best` that outputs a good tile's words for less than
   `(1 − g₀)` of rev1's credit. Known classes, and where this PR leaves them:
   * **The first-promotion skip** (your rating D on `TTOutPearlC`). rev1 takes the first promotion's FADD out of both
     the credit and W_ref (`first_adds`). The test replays the skip and finds it beats the bound under the pre-rev1
     credit at k = 256, and never under rev1's.
   * **Cross-group joint skips** (`joint_cross_group`), which TT_OUT carries. Per word, census tiles have up to 11
     atoms jointly skippable beyond the debit, and priced word by word that beats the bound. W1's H100 tensor-core
     fragments are 64 × ≥64 words (`h100S8Frags`), and on the census tiles the jointly skippable sets intersected over a
     tile are empty or fully debited. Is the whole-fragment rule sound here? Could an adversary choose operands, or a
     fragment layout (permuting rows into a fragment), so that a fragment's words share a skip set, while staying in the
     domain (the §5 noise floor and liveness)?
   * **Scalar paths.** Could an adversary compute only a word's non-skipped atoms with FP32 or INT32 instructions, at
     less than the tensor-core work they replace?
   * **INT8 (**`.s8` **fragments, m64nN for N = 64..256 step 16).** Is there an INT8 route to a good tile's E4M3 words
     cheaper than the E4M3 MMA, such as splitting E4M3 codes into INT8 parts and recombining exactly?
2. **Zero prices.** We claim `sha = fetch = 0` is the adversary's best case, and only that. Check the monotonicity proof
   for a W1 code path whose cost falls as a price rises; there should be none, since every price enters cost with a
   nonnegative coefficient. The honest side is priced separately (rev1's W_ref), so zero here makes the conjecture
   harder, not easier.
3. **The device model's scope.** `w1-complete-h100` says W1 at the data-sheet ratios is complete for the card.
   * Name an H100 or H200 instruction, or a sequence of them, that W1 overprices relative to Table 1, such as sparse
     tensor cores (2:4) or FP8 with FP16 accumulation.
   * Name a memory effect that W1 doesn't charge and that lets a program skip tensor-core work.
   * `CostModel.SimulatedBy` asks for one W1 program per program of `CM`, uniformly over oracle, workload, activations,
     state and salt. Is that too strong to be true of the card, which would leave the guarantee vacuous, or exactly what
     the transfer needs?
4. **The split itself.** Check that the two named assumptions together say no more than the old mixed premise
   intended. In particular: W1 makes no oracle call, so a `CM` whose runs read `H` beyond the counted queries isn't
   simulated, and the device model speaks only at the deployment's noise (SHA-512 under the live salt).

## The falsification test

`benchmarks/pouw/tests/test_tt_out_tile_h100_rev1.py` runs in `check` (the `verity-pouw-benchmarks` suite, about 2.5 s).
The lock's `shape.conjectures` entry names it as the conjecture's standing test.

* `test_first_promotion_skip_is_outside_rev1s_credit`:
  * On census-formed H100 tiles (hopper atom, G = 4, k = 1024, six families), the skip-first chain's ticket equals the
    honest one, word for word.
  * On domain shapes from k = 256 to 65536, the skip program at rev1's W_ref never fires the event under rev1's credit.
  * Under the pre-rev1 credit (credit + first_adds) it fires, so the test is shown to catch the old falsifier.
* `test_cross_group_joint_skips_stay_within_the_gap`:
  * With the products in hand, each word's greedy joint set is intersected over the tile, replayed on every word, and
    its undebited atoms priced against the tile's credit. The event never fires.
  * Priced word by word, it fires, so the test reaches the class and the bound rests on the fragment rule.

A new skip class you find belongs in this file as another test. A counterexample makes the conjecture falsified in the
lock (`status: falsified`), which refuses both new guarantees.
